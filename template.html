About this map

A working map of Midwest rivers that are set up for kayaking. Green rivers are runs where motorboats are prohibited (several Michigan rivers have formal bans) or simply can't operate (rapids, carry-in landings, shallow narrow channels), and that hold enough water to float a kayak in a normal May–September season — each card notes where that gets marginal. Blue rivers are the big shared waterways, shown for orientation without landing detail.

Click a river — in the list or on the map — and it lights up route-style from its first put-in (A) to its final take-out (B) while the map holds exactly where you are. Clicking a line on the map also opens that river's state and section in the side list. The only thing that moves the map is expanding a state header (each state has a fixed home view) or a card's "zoom to river" link. Click a selected river again (or tap empty map) to clear. Details open in the inspector at the bottom-left. Access points draw as small white dots that appear as you zoom in (a selected river shows its dots one zoom level earlier). On the map, kayak rivers draw in three dark purple shades by season (darkest = floats into Aug–Sept, lightest = spring–June) so they stand out against forested terrain; the side panel keeps its green season labels. Selecting any river glows it blue. State borders are tinted for orientation (WI green, IL red, IN tan, IA yellow, OH orange, MN purple, MI slate).

Live data

River lines load from OpenStreetMap (Overpass API) when the page opens, so they follow the real channel at any zoom. Flow readings come live from USGS gauges; each green river card shows current cfs against the run's documented minimum. If geometry can't load (no internet), simplified fallback lines are drawn instead.

Landings

Put-in / take-out points were compiled from published paddling guides and public land records: Miles Paddled, WisconsinRiverTrips, Wisconsin Trail Guide, the Kickapoo Valley Reserve guide, the Fabulous Fox Water Trail, Milwaukee Riverkeeper and county park pages. Each popup links its source. A few documented access points aren't pinned yet — they're listed in each river's card.

Coverage — v4

148 rivers across ten states (Missouri's Ozark rivers — Current, Jacks Fork, Eleven Point and friends — joined in v6): Wisconsin (regions SE/NE/W), Illinois (north and south of US-24), Michigan's Lower Peninsula (west and east), Kentucky, West Virginia, Ohio, Minnesota, Indiana, Iowa and Missouri — each new state a curated list of its most-paddled rivers. Put-in/take-out pins (347 so far) cover Wisconsin, Illinois and all of Michigan's Lower Peninsula after a full-run QC pass (v0.2) that verified extents, mileages and access order against DNR, USFS, water-trail and paddling-guide sources; pins, gauge links and sources for the remaining states are being added state by state — their flow ranges are estimates for now.

Drive distances & sort

The chip beside each river is the driving distance from your home base (default O'Hare — change it with the ⌂ button: pick an airport or drop a pin on the map; your choice is saved in the page URL). List chips start as road-factor estimates (~) and upgrade to true driving miles when you select a river; the card then also shows drive time. Color bands: green under 100 mi (day trip), amber 100–250 (weekend), purple 250+ (expedition). Within each section, rivers are sorted by bottom-out risk — all-season rivers first, then thru-July, then spring-only — with the longest run first inside each tier. Every river draws a dashed backup line instantly (hand-traced along its documented course where needed) so selection always highlights something, even offline; solid, exact geometry replaces it as it loads.

Outfitters & campgrounds

Color = season (green floats into Aug–Sept, sky blue thru July, light purple spring–June; navy = big shared rivers). Line style = rentals: DASHED rivers (with filled list icons) have a kayak-rental outfitter identified — most are draft research pending verification, with only the Pere Marquette and Pine linked in the detail window so far. Faint DOTTED rivers (hollow list icons) have no outfitter found yet: bring your own boat. The full outfitter roadmap lives in a companion spreadsheet during vetting.

Time estimates

The "X mi · ~Y hr" figures are deliberately conservative: beginner pace (~2 mph, 2.5 on the fast spring-fed rivers) at the low end of the flow range, and they do NOT include stops — real trips with breaks will run longer. Flow ranges show typical spring-high → late-summer-low readings; "min runnable" is the flow (in cfs) below which the run gets too shallow to enjoy.

Fine print

Rivers change: deadfall moves, access rules change, droughts happen. This map is a planning aid, not a guarantee — check gauges, outfitter reports and land ownership before you paddle. Some listed landings (Herb's, Wild Wolf Inn, Lost Falls, Ayers) are private and need permission; the Wolf below County M requires a Menominee tribal permit; Michigan's Pine and Pere Marquette require a USFS watercraft permit from Memorial Day weekend through Labor Day.
